Why it fails — and what it does not mean

The FAIL is on confinement only, and it names exactly two ports: 0x514 and 0x518, both written by the generic I/O helper FUN_00001bac. The values are literal immediates at real call sites, faithfully recovered from the binary — not inferred. The other five ports pass: 0x0 (legacy DMA range), CF8/CFC (PCI config range), and the MSR read is SEV_STATUS (0xC0010131, allowlisted). The device behind ports 0x514/0x518 has not been identified from the binary, so the allowlist has not been extended to cover them. Extending it is a separate decision that requires identifying that device first. This FAIL is the confinement check doing its job on genuine new findings — it is not a claim that the driver is malicious, and it is not hidden to keep the page green.

Provenance

Source: Ubuntu package ovmf-generic 2025.11-3ubuntu7.3, EDK2 2025.11. PE image, 52 functions. No BAR-relative findings: BAR bases arrive at runtime as hardware inputs.

This is a static proof tool. It does not execute firmware and does not claim to find all vulnerabilities. PASS does not mean firmware is secure.
